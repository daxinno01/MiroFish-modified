# Scenario Brief: DBS GenAI Anti-Scam Campaign

Company: DBS Bank
Industry: Financial Services / Banking
Brand Positioning: Singapore's largest and most tech-forward bank, highly integrated into daily life (PayNow, digibank). Positioned as secure, innovative, and deeply local.

Event:
In Q2 2026, DBS Bank releases a fully GenAI-created public educational video campaign to warn the public about sophisticated malware and phishing scams.

Video Details:
- Length: 3.5 minutes.
- Style: Hyper-realistic, life-like cinematic educational short. It depicts a relatable Singaporean family falling victim to a malicious app download.
- AI Disclosure: Highly visible. Features a prominent text banner and voiceover at 0:00 and 3:25 stating: "Transparency matters. The characters, voices, and scenarios in this video are 100% generated by Artificial Intelligence to simulate real-world scam tactics."
- Tools Used: Advanced text-to-video GenAI models (e.g., Sora/Veo tier) and AI voice cloning.
- Human Involvement: Strictly limited to prompt engineering and manual post-production editing (stitching clips and scene transitions). No human actors, set designers, or voiceover artists were employed.
- Message: "Scams are getting more sophisticated through AI and deepfakes. Here is how to spot them and transact securely."

Prediction Target:
Predict public reception over a 7-day period following the launch, focusing on sentiment shifts, the irony of using GenAI to fight GenAI scams, and the reaction from both vulnerable demographics (elderly) and critical demographics (creatives and tech youth).

# Prior Brand Sentiment & Historical Analogues

Brand Reputation (DBS Bank):
- Trust Level: Very high. DBS is viewed as too-big-to-fail and a staple of Singaporean life.
- Recent Tech Sentiment: While praised for digital innovation, DBS suffered several high-profile digibank app outages in 2023 and 2024. As a result, the public is highly sensitive to DBS's technology choices. There is a lingering narrative of "Fix your servers before spending on fancy AI."
- Anti-Scam Fatigue: Singaporeans are bombarded daily by SMS, print, and digital anti-scam warnings from the police and banks. Standard messaging is often ignored due to fatigue.

Comparable Historical Analogues:
1. Toys "R" Us Sora Ad (2024):
- Resulted in massive backlash from creatives calling it "soulless" and "creepy," overshadowing the brand's innovation narrative.
2. Under Armour AI Controversy (2024):
- Criticized for taking credit away from human directors.
3. Singapore Police Force (SPF) Anti-Scam Ads:
- Typically use real, local actors with high dramatic flair (often becoming viral for being "campy" or relatable). An AI video will be directly compared to these beloved human-acted ads.
