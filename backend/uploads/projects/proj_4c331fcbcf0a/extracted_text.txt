# Scenario Brief: DBS GenAI Anti-Scam Campaign

Company: DBS Bank
Industry: Financial Services / Banking
Brand Positioning: Singapore's largest and most tech-forward bank, highly integrated into daily life (PayNow, digibank). Positioned as secure, innovative, and deeply local.

Event:
In Q2 2026, DBS Bank releases a fully GenAI-created public educational video campaign to warn the public about sophisticated malware and phishing scams.

Video Details:
- Length: 3.5 minutes.
- Style: Hyper-realistic, life-like cinematic educational short. It depicts a relatable Singaporean family falling victim to a malicious app download.
- AI Disclosure: Highly visible. Features a prominent text banner and voiceover at 0:00 and 3:25 stating: "Transparency matters. The characters, voices, and scenarios in this video are 100% generated by Artificial Intelligence to simulate real-world scam tactics."
- Tools Used: Advanced text-to-video GenAI models (e.g., Sora/Veo tier) and AI voice cloning.
- Human Involvement: Strictly limited to prompt engineering and manual post-production editing (stitching clips and scene transitions). No human actors, set designers, or voiceover artists were employed.
- Message: "Scams are getting more sophisticated through AI and deepfakes. Here is how to spot them and transact securely."

Prediction Target:
Predict public reception over a 7-day period following the launch, focusing on sentiment shifts, the irony of using GenAI to fight GenAI scams, and the reaction from both vulnerable demographics (elderly) and critical demographics (creatives and tech youth).

=== 04_video_content_and_risks.md ===
# Video Content and Narrative Risk Factors

Specific Visuals:
- The video shows an AI-generated elderly woman accidentally downloading a third-party app to buy cheap durians, leading to her screen being mirrored and funds drained.
- The visuals are highly polished. Thanks to heavy prompt engineering and rigorous human vetting, the main characters and foreground actions are flawless (no six-fingered hands or garbled main text). However, there is one very subtle temporal artifact: at the 1:14 mark, an out-of-focus pedestrian in the background slightly morphs and disappears for a split second. It is barely noticeable to the average viewer but easily caught if analyzed frame-by-frame.

Core Risk Vectors:
- The Irony Risk: The public may fixate on the hypocrisy of using deepfake/GenAI technology to warn the public about scammers who use deepfake/GenAI technology.
- The Empathy Deficit: Because the victim losing her life savings is a synthetic AI avatar, viewers may feel zero emotional resonance, defeating the educational purpose.
- Labor Backlash: High probability of local production houses complaining about lost contracts.

# Media Ecosystem & Editorial Framing

## Reliable / Mainstream Media (The "Authority" Nodes)
These outlets are trusted by the older generation, professionals, and regulators. They will likely frame the campaign around its utility and the technology behind it.

1. **The Straits Times (ST)**
- **Audience:** Broad national audience, older demographics, government, professionals.
- **Expected Framing:** Objective, civic-minded. Will focus on the anti-scam initiative and quote DBS executives or the police.
- **Simulated Headline:** "DBS launches fully AI-generated educational video to combat rising malware scams."
- **Simulation Role:** Reassures the elderly demographic and provides official legitimacy to the campaign.

2. **CNA (Channel NewsAsia)**
- **Audience:** Professionals, regional viewers, high-income earners.
- **Expected Framing:** Focuses on both the civic message and the business/tech angle of using GenAI.
- **Simulated Headline:** "No human actors: Inside DBS’s push to use GenAI for real-time scam education."

## Viral / Social-First Local Media (The "Amplifier" Nodes)
These sites monitor platforms like Reddit (r/singapore) and TikTok. They will aggressively amplify the memes, the irony, and any public backlash. They are the primary news source for Gen Z and Millennials.

3. **Mothership.sg**
- **Audience:** Mass youth, Gen Z, Millennials. Highly viral.
- **Expected Framing:** Snarky, highly attuned to internet culture. Will aggregate Reddit comments and highlight visual AI errors or the "irony" of the campaign.
- **Simulated Headline:** "DBS made a 100% AI anti-scam video. S'poreans are distracted by the 'uncanny' durian auntie."
- **Simulation Role:** Bridges the gap between niche Reddit debates and the mainstream public, causing negative sentiment to go viral.

4. **AsiaOne & MUSTShareNews (MSNews)**
- **Audience:** Heartlanders, general social media users.
- **Expected Framing:** Sensationalist, focusing on outrage or emotional reactions.
- **Simulated Headline:** "‘Profit so high but use AI?’: Local creatives slam DBS's new AI-generated scam ad."

## Niche & Cultural Media (The "Think-Piece" Nodes)
These outlets shape the opinions of the intelligentsia, creatives, and industry professionals.

5. **Rice Media**
- **Audience:** Cultural commentators, creatives, progressives.
- **Expected Framing:** Long-form critical analysis of the societal implications. Will likely attack the lack of human empathy in an AI ad about losing life savings.
- **Simulated Headline:** "Can an AI Auntie make us care about scams? The soulless reality of corporate GenAI."
- **Simulation Role:** Validates the anger of the "Local Creative Professionals" demographic.

6. **Tech in Asia / Vulcan Post**
- **Audience:** Startup founders, tech workers, corporate strategists.
- **Expected Framing:** Pragmatic, analyzing the cost-efficiency vs. brand risk.
- **Simulated Headline:** "DBS’s GenAI gamble: A scalable solution to scam education or a brand risk?"

=== 03_prior_sentiment_and_analogues.md ===
# Prior Brand Sentiment & Historical Analogues

Brand Reputation (DBS Bank):
- Trust Level: Very high. DBS is viewed as too-big-to-fail and a staple of Singaporean life.
- Recent Tech Sentiment: While praised for digital innovation, DBS suffered several high-profile digibank app outages in 2023 and 2024. As a result, the public is highly sensitive to DBS's technology choices. There is a lingering narrative of "Fix your servers before spending on fancy AI."
- Anti-Scam Fatigue: Singaporeans are bombarded daily by SMS, print, and digital anti-scam warnings from the police and banks. Standard messaging is often ignored due to fatigue.

Comparable Historical Analogues:
1. Toys "R" Us Sora Ad (2024):
- Resulted in massive backlash from creatives calling it "soulless" and "creepy," overshadowing the brand's innovation narrative.
2. Under Armour AI Controversy (2024):
- Criticized for taking credit away from human directors.
3. Singapore Police Force (SPF) Anti-Scam Ads:
- Typically use real, local actors with high dramatic flair (often becoming viral for being "campy" or relatable). An AI video will be directly compared to these beloved human-acted ads.

=== 02_stakeholder_map.md ===
# Stakeholder Map & Micro-Demographics

Stakeholders:
1. Vulnerable Elderly (Seniors / Ah Mas / Uncles):
- Likely concern: Highly fearful of losing life savings.
- Tech literacy: Low. May not fully comprehend what "100% generated by AI" means, leading to potential confusion over whether the scam shown is real.

2. Tech-Savvy Youth & Gen Z (Reddit r/singapore users):
- Likely positive frame: Appreciates the hyper-realistic simulation of how malware actually takes over a phone.
- Likely negative frame: Quick to point out "uncanny valley" visual glitches; might turn the AI characters into local memes.

3. Local Creative Professionals (Filmmakers, Actors, Ad Agencies):
- Likely concern: Replacement of local human creative labor by a highly profitable bank.
- Likely negative frame: "DBS makes billions in profit but cheapskates on hiring local Singaporean actors for a nationwide campaign."
- Influence level: High on LinkedIn, Instagram, and local media op-eds.

4. Cybersecurity Experts & Tech Analysts:
- Likely positive frame: Acknowledges that using GenAI allows the bank to rapidly iterate and produce new videos as scam tactics change week-by-week.

5. Regulators (MAS - Monetary Authority of Singapore):
- Priority: Ensuring public trust in the banking system isn't eroded by confusing communications.
